— Biography

Yang Jianbo's work occupies a rare space between documentary and dream — rooted in the landscapes of Yunnan and Tibet, yet charged with an interior logic that transcends geography.

Born in 1972 in Kunming, Yunnan Province, Yang Jianbo graduated from the Fine Arts College of Southwest University in 1991. He currently serves as Professor and Department Chair at the School of Art and Design, Yunnan University, where he also supervises graduate students in oil painting.

His work has been selected for the 3rd National Oil Painting Exhibition, and the 10th, 12th, 13th, and 14th National Fine Arts Exhibitions — among the most prestigious institutional acknowledgments in contemporary Chinese painting. In 2023, he received a National Arts Fund grant for fine arts creation.

His paintings are held in the collections of Jiangsu Art Museum, China News Service Yunnan Branch, and numerous private collections internationally.

— Thematic Series
01

Plateau Pastoral

Large-scale oil paintings rooted in the landscapes of Yunnan and the Tibetan plateau — herders, cattle, and open terrain rendered with quiet monumentality. Works such as Pasture, Waza and the Bull, and The Shepherd and the Blue Mountain place the human figure in a charged relationship with land and animal.

02

The Twenty-Four Solar Terms

A sequence of oil paintings drawn from the ancient Chinese agricultural calendar — Awakening of Insects, Autumnal Equinox, Cold Dew, Start of Winter, Minor Cold, Winter Solstice, and others. Each work distills a seasonal turning point into colour and atmosphere, tracing the year's slow rhythm through the plateau light of Yunnan.

03

Flying Fish and Birds · 飞鱼和鸟

A series of eight pencil drawings on paper (18×42 cm each) in which flying fish and birds share an impossible sky — part natural observation, part surrealist vision. The elongated horizontal format gives each work the quality of a scroll, slowing the eye across creatures suspended between water, air, and the drawn line.

04

Machines · 拖拉机 & 红色机器

Two watercolour postcard series — Tractor (2016) and Red Machine (2021) — depicting agricultural and industrial machines as intimate collectible objects. Painted at postcard scale and designed to stand on a desk, they reframe tools of labour as quiet still-life subjects, seen with affection rather than ideology.
